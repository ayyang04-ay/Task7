# 1. Recommendation

**Keep the program in the portfolio, on three conditions.**

1. **One lead.** Take PSLN-344 forward. Stop active development of PSLN-212 and keep it as the comparator arm.
2. **One indication.** Present the asset as what the data show it to be: an adjuvant, given through the nose together with inactivated influenza vaccine, for older adults. Every efficacy experiment in the dataset is an influenza vaccination-and-challenge experiment in mice. The data do not support a stand-alone STING drug: PSLN-344 without vaccine protected 0/7 animals [S15-H].
3. **One gate.** Approve a six-week audit of the dataset and then Stage 1, a package of six mouse and human-cell studies that ends at a go/no-go gate in October 2027. Commit to IND-enabling work only if that gate is passed.

## Why keep it

- **It is the only formulation we have that protects aged animals.** Across 11 lethal-challenge experiments, 68 of 80 (85%) aged mice survived after vaccine with PSLN-344, against 11 of 80 (14%) after vaccine with PSLN-212 and 1 of 80 (1%) after vaccine with empty liposome (sham). PSLN-344 was ahead in 11 of 11 experiments (Figure 1). Resolving every discrepancy in the dataset against PSLN-344 still leaves 66 of 80 (82%) against 15 of 70 (21%) (Section 9).
- **It restores the antibody response that age removes.** Fourteen days after one dose, hemagglutination-inhibition (HAI) titers were 8.0-fold (H1N1) and 9.2-fold (H3N2) higher than with PSLN-212, and reached 76% and 87% of the titers in young animals given PSLN-212, the benchmark for a full response [S03-C, S03-G] (Figure 2).
- **Protection is broad, fast, and still present at two months.** Against strains whose hemagglutinin did not match the vaccine, 39/49 survived versus 6/49 [S06-A to S06-G]. When challenge came 2 to 3 days after the single dose, 22/24 versus 5/24 [S03-J, S06-B, S06-D]. At 60 days, 14/21 versus 1/21; for reference, 17 of 21 (81%) unchallenged animals of the same age survived the same two weeks [S06-E, S06-F, S06-G].
- **The advantage over PSLN-212 is specific to age, and that defines where we are differentiated.** In young animals both formulations protected 24/24 against mismatched strains, against 0/23 with sham [S09-B, S09-D, S09-F]. In aged animals only PSLN-344 did. PSLN-212 therefore has no case as the lead.

## Why only conditionally

- **The dataset is not yet fit to put in front of the committee.** I logged 20 discrepancies (Section 9). Two headline experiments have group labels that conflict with their paired body-weight tables [S03-I, S03-J]. One derived table has rotated column labels, which reverses what it says about PSLN-344 [S03-B, S03-F]. Identical body-weight series appear in unrelated experiments. None of these changes the direction of a conclusion above, but the committee will find them. Each should be closed against raw records, or disclosed, before the review.
- **We cannot yet say which ingredient is doing the work.** The two formulations differ only in Mn²⁺, and no experiment gave Mn²⁺ without our agonist. In the same liposome our agonist outperforms the natural ligand cGAMP by about 2-fold on interferon induction and on 5 of 6 antibody readouts [S01-A, S03-A to S03-H]; adding Mn²⁺ then adds 8- to 19-fold on antibody. Whether our agonist is still needed once Mn²⁺ is present has not been tested.
- **The safety of that ingredient by this route is untested.** There is no measurement of manganese in any tissue, no histopathology, and nothing after repeat dosing. What exists is reassuring but thin: body weight dipped to 96.7% of baseline and recovered by day 10 [S08-D], and brain cytokine transcripts stayed within 1.2-fold of the level seen with vaccine and empty liposome, while the cholera-toxin positive control rose 2.7- to 5.5-fold [S10-A]. Against that, lung *Tnf* and *Il6* were still 8.4- and 7.2-fold above baseline at the last time point measured [S09-A, second panel; see D5 in Appendix A], and the neutrophil data cannot be used [S11-B].
- **Everything is in mice, and four links to a human product are missing.** The mechanism starts in the deep lung [S01-B, S01-C], which a nasal spray generally does not reach in people (*external*). No human cells have been tested. The antigen is in-house whole inactivated virus, not a licensed vaccine. And there is no comparison with the high-dose or adjuvanted vaccines that older adults already receive.
- **The gap to young animals is narrowed, not closed.** In the 10 experiments of Figure 1 that ran both arms, survival was 58/70 vs 69/70 (aged PSLN-344 versus young benchmark), with most of the shortfall at day 60.

## What would turn this into a recommendation to drop

Any one of the following, each of which is tested in Stage 0 or Stage 1:

- the audit removes the difference between PSLN-344 and PSLN-212;
- a particle carrying Mn²⁺ but no agonist performs as well as PSLN-344;
- manganese accumulates in brain or olfactory tissue, or causes lesions, at the intended dose;
- the effect is lost when the dose is delivered in a way people can take, and an inhaled product is not acceptable to us;
- the agonist does not activate the common human variants of STING;
- PSLN-344 shows no advantage over the enhanced vaccines already used in older adults.

Four points follow.

1. **In aged animals PSLN-344 is better on every efficacy and immune measure, by a wide margin.** Tolerability looks similar on body weight, but lung cytokine transcripts at the later time point are higher with PSLN-344 (*Tnf* 8.4 versus 5.2-fold, *Il6* 7.2 versus 1.9-fold) [S09-A].
2. **In young animals Mn²⁺ buys nothing we can measure.** Survival is identical. At the one late time point tested, PSLN-344 animals regained weight more slowly (one experiment). Mn²⁺ does raise and prolong the interferon response in young animals, which is added stimulation without added benefit. If a product for younger adults were ever wanted, the Mn²⁺-free particle would be the simpler candidate; that is the one reason to keep PSLN-212 on file.
3. **Most of the value sits in the Mn²⁺ step.** In aged mice, H1N1 HAI titers step from 8 (sham) to 23 (cGAMP liposome) to 46 (PSLN-212) to 368 (PSLN-344) [S03-C]. The committee will ask whether we own that last step. The workbook cannot answer; Study A and a freedom-to-operate review (Section 6) are designed to.
4. **The vehicle's contribution is delivery, and it is only partly tested.** Liposomal dye reached 15.8% of type II lung epithelial cells against 0.4% for free dye [S01-B], and uptake by alveolar macrophages fell from 43.1% to 12.7% without surfactant proteins [S01-C]. The empty vehicle has no adjuvant effect of its own (sham, 1/80 survivors). But no experiment gave the agonist without the vehicle, so what the vehicle adds to efficacy is inferred, not measured. Study A adds that arm.

# 4. Mechanism of action and biomarkers

## Mechanism

Figure 4 assembles the mechanism from the workbook. In short: the liposome delivers the agonist to alveolar macrophages and on to the lung epithelium, and the data are consistent with transfer through gap junctions. The resulting interferon and GM-CSF signals recruit monocytes and turn them into dendritic cells, which carry antigen to the lymph node and start the antibody and CD8 T-cell responses. Aging weakens the interferon signal, so in aged animals given PSLN-212 the monocytes accumulate as macrophages instead (one small experiment [S07-A]), regulatory T cells stay high in the lung (6.7%, against 3.0% with PSLN-344 and 1.8% in the young benchmark [S04-D]) and the response stalls. Adding Mn²⁺ restores the signal.

![**Figure 4.** Mechanism of action with the supporting numbers at each step. Blue boxes are supported by an intervention experiment; orange boxes only by comparing formulations.](/tmp/outputs/figures/fig4_mechanism.png){width=6.3in}

How strong each link is:

- **Shown by intervention.** Uptake needs surfactant proteins (43.1% of alveolar macrophages took up liposome in wild-type mice, 12.7% in knockouts) [S01-C]. Blocking gap junctions cuts monocyte recruitment from 7.3% to 3.1% [S11-C]. A CCR2 antagonist removes the monocytes (9.9% to 3.3%) [S07-B] and with them the CD8 effector response (6.8% to 1.4%, in young animals) [S02-I]. Bone marrow lacking the interferon receptor cannot produce monocyte-derived dendritic cells (19.7% to 2.1%) unless GM-CSF is supplied (12.4%) [S02-H]; monocyte entry itself did not clearly depend on that receptor (21.3% versus 15.4%, three animals per arm) [S12-A]. Blocking cell migration stops T-cell priming in the lymph node (30.9% to 6.8%) but not in the lung (59.0% versus 55.1%) [S05-C].
- **Shown only by comparing formulations.** This covers the claim that Mn²⁺ is the cause of the higher interferon and GM-CSF, and every step from T-cell help onward.
- **Not tested.** The workbook has no arm with Mn²⁺ and no agonist, and no animals lacking STING. It does not show whether antibody, CD8 T cells or both are needed for protection, or why protection appears within 2 to 3 days, which is too early for antibody, yet requires the vaccine antigen [S15-H].
- **Weak point.** Co-stimulatory molecules on lymph-node dendritic cells reach only 29% (CD40) and 38% (CD86) of the young benchmark [S02-D]. If the aged response is still limited somewhere, the data point here.
- **Unusable.** The experiment meant to show which macrophage type drives regulatory T cells cannot be read, because both arms carry the same label [S07-C].

Three cautions:

1. **These are group-level associations.** The animals that gave samples are not the animals that were challenged. Study A bleeds every animal the day before challenge, so that we can test whether an individual titer predicts an individual outcome.
2. **The usual HAI threshold of 1:40 does not discriminate here.** 5/5 aged PSLN-212 animals reached 40 against H1N1 [S03-C], yet that regimen protected 0/7 against the matched virus [S03-I] (or 3/7 on the other reading of that experiment; D2 in Section 9). Every PSLN-344 animal was at 160 or above and every PSLN-212 animal at 80 or below. I would use 160 as the working threshold in mice until Study A refines it.
3. **Use the IgG2a:IgG1 ratio computed from the raw subclass titers,** not the printed ratio tables [S03-B, S03-F], whose column labels are rotated (D1 in Section 9).

# 5. Target population, for the market-research check

**Primary population: adults aged 65 and over who are offered seasonal influenza vaccination.**

Why this group, from our data:

- The advantage of PSLN-344 over PSLN-212 exists only in aged animals: 68/80 versus 11/80 survived in aged mice, 24/24 versus 24/24 in young mice.
- The aged mice were 20 months or older. On the usual lifespan conversion that corresponds to people in their late 50s to late 60s and beyond (*external*: Jackson Laboratory equivalence, 18 to 24 months is roughly 56 to 69 years).
- It works in animals that had a previous influenza infection (12/14 survived) [S06-E and S06-F, rows 71 to 98], which is the situation of every adult.

**Possible extension: adults aged 50 to 64.** In 14-month-old mice, roughly the human mid-40s, PSLN-212 had already failed in animals without prior infection (0/14) while PSLN-344 protected 12/14 [same blocks]. So the age at which the Mn²⁺ formulation becomes necessary lies below 14 months in mice. Study F locates it. Until then, treat 50 to 64 as upside and not as base case.

**Secondary use, bought by governments and not by the seasonal market: outbreak and pandemic response in older adults.** The support is protection 2 to 3 days after a single dose (22/24), including against wild-type H5N1 (10/10) [S03-J, S06-B, S06-D].

**Adults under 50: a later option, not part of the core case.** Both formulations gave young mice full protection against mismatched strains (24/24 each, against 0/23 with sham) [S09-B, S09-D, S09-F], so a broader-acting intranasal vaccine could have value in this group too. But the case there is breadth, not rescue of a failing response; PSLN-344 has no advantage over PSLN-212; a cGAMP liposome also protected young animals (7/7, matched challenge) [S13-E]; and existing vaccines already work in young adults (*external*). Keep this group out of the base case.

**Outside the addressable market on current data:**

- children: there are no data;
- any use without a vaccine antigen: adjuvant alone protected 0/7 [S15-H];
- any indication other than influenza, including oncology: there are no data.

**Deductions to apply inside the 65-and-over group** (assumptions that Studies B and C and the clinical program will confirm or remove):

- People with chronic lung disease such as asthma or COPD, at least at first, because the product acts in the airway and on current evidence needs to reach the lung.
- People in whom manganese clearance or neurological vulnerability is a concern, such as chronic liver disease or neurodegenerative disease, pending Study C.

One caveat applies to the whole population: all animals tested were female. Study A adds males.

**How the product reaches the patient.** It is an adjuvant and is only useful combined with an influenza antigen. The sizing should therefore be framed as a share of enhanced influenza vaccines for older adults, and should state whether we supply an adjuvant to a vaccine partner or a complete vaccine.

**Inputs market research will need that are not in our dataset:** number of people aged 65 and over by market; seasonal vaccination coverage in that group; the share already receiving enhanced vaccines (high-dose, adjuvanted, recombinant) and their price; acceptance of a nasal or inhaled route; and the 50 to 64 population as a separate line.

For orientation only (*external*, quoted from memory of public statistics, to be replaced by market research): about 60 million people aged 65 and over in the United States with seasonal influenza vaccine coverage near 70%, roughly 95 to 100 million in the European Union, and about 36 million in Japan.

## Formulation and intellectual-property workstream (December 2026 to September 2027)

## Gate 1, October 2027

Proceed to Stage 2 only if all of the following hold:

1. The audited dataset still shows the PSLN-344 advantage (Stage 0).

**Does any of this change the recommendation?** No, for three reasons.

1. With every survival-versus-weight conflict resolved against PSLN-344 and the disputed PSLN-212 arm of the H5N1 experiment removed, survival is 66 of 80 (82%) versus 15 of 70 (21%).
2. Restricting to the three experiments in which the survival table and the weight table agree for every arm and the design is fully recorded [S06-A, S06-B, S06-F], it is 16 of 21 (76%) versus 2 of 21 (10%).
3. The antibody results come from raw titers in blocks whose group sizes match their labels and in which the only repeated values are coincident dilution steps [S03-A, S03-C to S03-E, S03-G, S03-H, S13-A to S13-D].

What the discrepancies do change is how much weight any single experiment can bear. They are the reason the first step I propose is an audit.

**Limits of the dataset that are not errors:**

- All animals are female; there are no male data.
- Group sizes are 2 to 10; most immunology blocks have 5 animals and most challenge arms 7.
- The workbook holds one set of values per block; it does not say whether experiments were repeated, randomized or scored blind.
- Every efficacy experiment used a single adjuvant dose (20 µg) and a single administration.
- The antigen throughout is in-house formalin-inactivated whole virus, not a licensed split, subunit or recombinant vaccine.
- Particle size, charge, morphology, Mn content and stability are described as measured in the annotation, but no such data are in the workbook.
- There is no lung virus load, no mucosal IgA, no histopathology and no measurement of manganese in any tissue.

# Appendix A. Discrepancy log

Each entry gives the evidence, why it matters, how I handled it in this analysis, and what the audit should close. Rank reflects how far the discrepancy could move a number the committee sees. The same log, with one row per entry, is in the sheet "Integrity_log" of `PSLN_evidence_tables.xlsx`.

**D1. IgG2a:IgG1 tables carry rotated column labels** (High; S03-B, S03-F vs S13-A to S13-D). The per-animal IgG2a/IgG1 quotients recomputed from the raw subclass titers [S13-A/B, S13-C/D] reproduce the printed values of S03-B and S03-F only after shifting the column headers by one position: the column printed as PSLN-344 holds the PSLN-212 animals, the column printed as PSLN-212(Y) holds the PSLN-344 animals, and the column printed as PSLN-212 holds the young animals. The raw-block labels are the ones that agree with the total-IgG rank order in S03-A and S03-E. *Why it matters:* PSLN-344 would show an IgG2a:IgG1 ratio of 0.54 (H1N1) and 0.46 (H3N2), i.e. lower than PSLN-212, instead of 1.85 and 2.30. *What I did:* Ratios recomputed from the raw subclass blocks; S03-B and S03-F not used. *For the audit:* Correct the headers of S03-B and S03-F against the ELISA plate records.

**D2. Matched-strain challenge: survival table and weight table disagree on groups and on PSLN-212 outcome** (High; S03-I vs S13-F). S13-F is annotated as the body weights of the S03-I animals, but its groups are PSLN-212(Y), Sham, PSLN-212, PSLN-344 and Naive, whereas S03-I lists Sham, CMP-204, PSLN-212, PSLN-344 and PSLN-212(Y). In S03-I all 7 PSLN-212 animals die by day 6; in S13-F 3 of 7 PSLN-212 animals regain weight and 4 of 7 carry a repeated value from day 10. The S13-F 'Naive' pattern (all lost by day 8) resembles the S03-I 'PSLN-212' column, and the S13-F 'PSLN-212' pattern resembles the S03-I 'CMP-204' column (3/7 alive). *Why it matters:* The PSLN-212 result in the matched-strain challenge is either 0/7 or 3/7, and a CMP-204 arm either exists (3/7) or does not. PSLN-344 is 7/7 in both tables. *What I did:* Survival table used as printed in the pooled analysis; worst-case analysis uses 3/7 for PSLN-212 (PSLN-344 7/7 vs 3/7 alone gives p = 0.07). The comparison with CMP-204 on survival is not relied on. *For the audit:* Establish from animal-room records which five groups were in this experiment and re-export both tables with animal IDs.

**D3. Wild-type H5N1 challenge: groups differ between tables; n and one time value inconsistent** (High; S03-J vs S13-G). S03-J lists Sham, PSLN-212, PSLN-344; S13-G (same animals per the annotation) lists PSLN-344, Sham, Naive. The S13-G 'Naive' arm (1 of 10 recovering, early losses) resembles the S03-J 'Sham' column (1/10); the S13-G 'Sham' arm (4 of 10 recovering) resembles the S03-J 'PSLN-212' column (3/10). S03-J is labeled n=9 but has 10 rows per group; S13-G is labeled n=10. One PSLN-344 row in S03-J has an event code but no day. The annotation gives 22 days of observation; survivors are recorded at day 21. *Why it matters:* A PSLN-212 arm may not have been part of the wild-type H5N1 experiment at all, in which case it says nothing about PSLN-344 versus PSLN-212. *What I did:* PSLN-344 result (10/10) used; the row without a day treated as alive at day 21. The two control arms are reported without relying on their identity. The worst-case analysis drops the PSLN-212 arm of this block. *For the audit:* Confirm group identities and n (9 or 10) from BSL-3 records; supply the missing time value.

**D4. 'Untreated' animals in the day-60 blocks were not challenged** (High; S06-E, S06-F, S06-G (rows 34-105); S15-E, S15-F, S15-G). Per the annotation these animals received no vaccine, no adjuvant and no virus. 4/21 died during the 14-day window, with recorded body weights staying within 94-102% of baseline. *Why it matters:* Read as infected controls, these animals (17 of 21 (81%) alive) would make PSLN-344 (14/21 alive) look no better than no treatment. *What I did:* Used as the estimate of background mortality in animals of this age, not as a challenge control. Challenged controls are the Sham arms. *For the audit:* Rename the group (for example "unchallenged, age-matched") in the locked dataset.

**D5. Second cytokine panel carries the same '16h' label as the first** (Medium; S09-A (columns X to AS) vs S01-A). Both S09-A panels are labeled '16h'. The Ifnb1 group means of the second panel match the 72 h data of S01-A (root-mean-square difference 0.011 log10 units) and not the 16 h data (0.88). *Why it matters:* The later time point would be missed, and with it the only information on how quickly lung cytokines resolve. *What I did:* Second panel treated as a 72 h time point; every statement that depends on this says so. *For the audit:* Confirm the time point from the qPCR run records and relabel.

**D6. Mixed scales for the same measurand** (Medium; S09-A vs S01-A, S08-A). S09-A stores log10 fold-change; S01-A and S08-A store linear fold-change for the same transcript. Titers are stored on a linear scale. *Why it matters:* Reading S09-A as linear would understate induction by orders of magnitude (a stored value of 2.77 means 590-fold, not 2.77-fold). *What I did:* S09-A back-transformed; titers and fold-changes analyzed on the log scale (geometric means). *For the audit:* State scale in every block header.

**D7. Neutrophil block shares values with the alveolar-macrophage block** (High; S11-A vs S11-B). 35 of 100 cells are identical between the two blocks; the first replicate is identical in 19 of 20 group-by-time cells and the second in 15 of 20. The annotation says the two blocks came from different lung preparations. *Why it matters:* Neutrophil influx is a basic lung-safety readout; as delivered, the block cannot support any statement about it. *What I did:* S11-B excluded. The workbook therefore contains no usable neutrophil data for either formulation. *For the audit:* Re-export S11-B from the flow-cytometry files.

**D8. Animals that left the study are encoded two ways in the body-weight blocks** (Medium; S13-F, S13-G, S15-A to S15-O). Some groups use blank cells after the last weighing (for example Sham in S15-A to S15-O). Others repeat the last value unchanged to day 14 (for example PSLN-212 in S15-A to S15-G, and the Sham and Naive groups of S13-G). *Why it matters:* Group means computed from the cells as printed would treat dead animals as stable at about 80% of baseline. *What I did:* Trailing repeated values treated as end of observation (three identical final cells, or two below 90%); post-challenge weight summaries use survivors only. *For the audit:* Store one row per animal with an explicit status column.

**D9. Identical body-weight series recur in different experiments** (High; S15-C/S15-I; S09-E/S15-M; S15-A/S15-M/S15-O; S09-C). 40 of 42 cells (days 2-12) are identical between the PSLN-344 arm of S15-C (challenge on day 14) and the vaccine + PSLN-344 arm of S15-I (challenge on day 3). Six of the seven young C57BL/6 trajectories in S15-M share four to seven of their seven post-baseline values with young BALB/c PSLN-344 trajectories in S09-E; two PSLN-344 trajectories of S15-A (BALB/c) largely reappear in S15-M (C57BL/6) and in part in S15-O (Swiss Webster); the day-4 Sham row of S15-A equals the day-4 PSLN-212 row of S15-M; the day-12 row of S09-C is identical in the PSLN-212Y and PSLN-344Y groups (8 of 8 values). *Why it matters:* Body-weight curves would be presented as independent confirmation in three strains when part of the data is shared between blocks. *What I did:* Body weight used only as supporting evidence; no conclusion rests on it. Survival tables are the primary record. *For the audit:* Re-export every body-weight block from the balance records; this is the largest audit item.

**D10. Survival and weight tables differ by one or two animals** (Medium; S06-C/S15-C, S06-D/S15-D, S06-E/S15-E, S06-G/S15-G, S15-L/S15-M, S09-F/S09-G). PSLN-344: 7/7 alive in S06-D but two animals carry repeated values from day 8 in S15-D; 5/7 in S06-E vs 6/7 in S15-E; 4/7 in S06-G vs 5 to 7 of 7 in S15-G. PSLN-212: 2/7 vs 1/7 (S06-C/S15-C), 0/7 vs 1/7 (S06-E/S15-E). Young benchmark: 7/7 vs 5/7 (S06-D/S15-D), 6/7 vs 7/7 (S15-L/S15-M). S09-F has 7 Sham rows; S09-G has 8 Sham animals. *Why it matters:* Individual experiment results could move by one or two animals in either direction. *What I did:* Survival tables used as printed. A worst-case analysis takes, for each arm, whichever table is less favorable to PSLN-344: 66 of 80 (82%) vs 15 of 70 (21%). *For the audit:* Reconcile each animal across the two tables.

**D11. Dye-uptake block with Mn-containing particle has a header order that conflicts with S01-B** (Medium; S08-C vs S01-B). For the same condition (liposomal dye without Mn, type I epithelial cells) S01-B gives 3.8% and S08-C gives 13.8%. If the S08-C columns are read in the order type II / type I for the plain particle, then type II / type I for the Mn particle, the two blocks agree (15.8% and 3.8% vs 15.8% and 3.8%). *Why it matters:* As printed, adding Mn²⁺ would cut delivery to type II cells from 15.8% to 3.8%; on the reconciled order it is 15.8% vs 13.8%. *What I did:* Not used for any claim. # Appendix C. Methods

**Parsing.** Each block was defined by hand from the printed layout, so that every value keeps its cell address. All 6,819 numeric cells are accounted for (`01_parse_workbook.py`). Labels were taken as printed; nothing was relabeled.

**Survival.** Time-to-event blocks store one row per animal with a day and a code. Comparison with the paired body-weight blocks confirms that 1 means death or humane endpoint on that day and 0 means alive at the end of observation. Survival proportions carry exact (Clopper-Pearson) 95% confidence intervals. Two-arm comparisons use the two-sided Fisher exact test. The pooled comparison is stratified by experiment: a Mantel-Haenszel difference in survival, and a log-rank test whose reference distribution comes from permuting labels within each experiment (200,000 permutations). Pooling across experiments with different viruses and intervals is a summary, not a claim that the experiments are replicates; Figure 1 shows each one (`06_survival.py`). The stratified log-rank statistic, the pooled Fisher test and one titer ratio were recomputed independently in R and agree (`crosscheck_in_R.R`).

**Titers and fold-changes.** These were analyzed on the log scale. Summaries are geometric means and ratios of geometric means, with 95% confidence intervals from Welch's t-test. HAI values recorded as 0 were set to 5, half the lowest dilution tested. S09-A is stored as log10 and was back-transformed (`07_immunogenicity_moa.py`, `08_safety_tolerability.py`).

**Percentages from flow cytometry.** These are summarized as arithmetic means and compared with Welch's t-test; exact Mann-Whitney p-values are in the evidence workbook. With 5 animals per group the smallest attainable exact p-value is 0.008. Holm's method was applied across the 37 comparisons of PSLN-344 with PSLN-212 in aged animals.

**Body weight.** Trailing repeated values (three identical final cells, or two below 90%) and blank cells were treated as end of observation. Body weight was used as supporting evidence only (`03_weight_vs_survival.py`, `08_safety_tolerability.py`).

**Gap closed (Figure 3).** This is (PSLN-344 aged minus PSLN-212 aged) divided by (PSLN-212 young minus PSLN-212 aged), computed on the log scale for titers, ratios and fold-changes (`12_fig3_restoration.py`).

**Integrity checks.** The checks were: stated versus observed group size (`02_n_audit.py`); survival versus weight tables (`03_weight_vs_survival.py`); repeated sequences (`04_duplicate_scan.py`, `04b_weight_trajectory_dups.py`); derived versus raw tables and scale checks (`05_derived_value_checks.py`).

**Sample sizes for proposed studies.** These use the exact power of the two-sided Fisher test for survival, and a t-test on log2 titers for HAI with the spread observed in S03-C and S03-G (`09_biomarkers_power.py`).

**What was not done.** No value was imputed, relabeled or excluded as an outlier. No raw instrument file was available, so none of the discrepancies could be resolved, only bounded.
