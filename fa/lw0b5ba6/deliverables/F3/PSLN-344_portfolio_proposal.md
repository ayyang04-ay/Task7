# PSLN-344: portfolio review proposal

*Recommendation, next studies, target population, mechanism and biomarkers, grounded in the program dataset*

Prepared for the portfolio committee, 1 November 2026. Basis: psln_program_dataset.xlsx (15 sheets; all 6,120 observations parsed) and psln_program_annotation.pdf. Square-bracket references ([L..], [Q..]) point to the evidence ledger in Appendix A, which gives the workbook cells and analysis behind every number.


## 1. Recommendation

**Keep PSLN-344 in the portfolio, conditionally.** Re-scope it as an intranasal influenza-vaccine adjuvant for older adults, fund only work packages 0 to 2 of section 4, and hold any IND-enabling spend until they pass. If the asset you have in mind is the manganese-free drug-plus-liposome (PSLN-212), the data say drop it for this use.

> **The asset, as the data define it**
>
> - I have taken the asset to be **PSLN-344**: the STING agonist CDN-1807 plus manganese (Mn²⁺) in a surfactant-mimicking liposome, given intranasally.
> - All efficacy data are as a **vaccine adjuvant**, given with inactivated influenza vaccine. Alone it protected 0/7 animals [L16].
> - The same drug and liposome **without manganese** is the comparator PSLN-212, the arm that fails in aged animals [L02]. There is no manganese-only control.
> - All data are from mice; the BALB/c animals that carry most of the evidence were female. "Aged" means 20 months or older, "young" 7 to 8 weeks, "Sham" vaccine with empty liposome.

**Why keep it**

- **It turns a vaccine that fails in aged animals into one that protects.** Across ten experiments, 61/73 aged mice (84%) survived lethal influenza challenge after one intranasal dose of vaccine with PSLN-344, against 9/73 (12%) with PSLN-212 and 1/73 with vaccine plus empty liposome: a difference from PSLN-212 of 71 points (95% CI 57 to 80) [L01, L02, L03, L05].
- **The result survives the least favourable reading of the record problems** in section 9: 59/73 against 15/73 [L06].
- **Protection is broad and fast.** It held against viruses of a different subtype from the vaccine (32/42 against 4/42) [L09b], 2 to 3 days after the single dose (28/31) [L08], in two further mouse strains (6/7 each) [L14] and after prior influenza infection (12/14) [L15].
- **The immunology agrees.** PSLN-344 closes a median 71% of the gap between aged and young responses across 21 read-outs [L43].

**Why only conditionally**

- **The record is not audit-clean.** One experiment appears twice, nine survival counts disagree with their own weight tables and three blocks carry demonstrably wrong headers (section 9). None changes the conclusion, but a committee that checks will find them.
- **Attribution is untested.** With no manganese-only arm and no STING-deficient host, the data cannot show that the STING agonist, rather than the manganese, restores protection (section 6).
- **Nothing bridges to people:** no human-cell data, second species, licensed vaccine antigen or licensed comparator.
- **The safety package is thin.** Lung transcripts for the inflammatory cytokines TNF and IL-6 were still 8.4- and 7.2-fold above baseline at 72 h, the last time measured [L62]; the only central-nervous-system (CNS) data are three transcripts at one time point [L63]; manganese disposition was not measured.
- **The benefit is specific to aged hosts and fades.** Young animals are fully protected without manganese (24/24; with it 24/24) [L17]; aged survival falls from 41/45 within two weeks to 14/21 at day 60 [L11].

**Decision requested:** approve work packages 0 to 2, agree the stop rules in section 4 now, and review at Gate 1.

Three readings of these data matter for positioning. First, PSLN-212 is weak in aged animals but not inert (9/73 against 1/73 for empty liposome, p = 0.020) [L02b]; manganese raises an existing effect rather than creating one. Second, PSLN-344 does not fully reach the young benchmark (51/63 against 62/63 in the nine experiments that contain both arms) [L04b]. Third, the day-60 shortfall is probably real rather than old age: untreated, unchallenged animals of the same age lost 4 of 21 over the same two weeks [L18], but those animals left the study at 94.5 to 96.6% of starting weight, whereas the PSLN-344 animals that died had fallen to 77.5 to 85.3% [L20], the pattern of infection.

**Tolerability.** Three small blocks bear on it (Figure 4). After vaccination without challenge, weight dipped to 96.4% with PSLN-344 against 98.2% with vaccine alone (difference -1.8 points, 95% CI -3.7 to 0.1) and was back to 99.8% by day 10 [L60]. The early cytokine response in aged lung was no larger than the manganese-free formulation produces in young lung for Il6 and Ccl2 (0.6- and 0.4-fold of it) but was higher for Tnf (2.9-fold) and Il10 (4.0-fold) [L61]. By 72 h young lungs were back to baseline for Tnf (0.5-fold) while aged lungs on PSLN-344 were at 8.4-fold [L62]. Part of that is age, since aged lungs on PSLN-212 were at 5.2-fold, but there is no later time point to show that it resolves. In the central nervous system PSLN-344 did not differ from vaccine plus empty liposome on any of three transcripts, while cholera toxin, the positive control, gave 2.7- to 5.5-fold [L63]. That study could not exclude a two-fold rise in Tnf, and nothing was measured about manganese itself.

*Figure 4. Tolerability data. A: body weight after intranasal vaccination without challenge. B: lung cytokine transcripts at 16 h and 72 h. C: central-nervous-system transcripts at 36 h.*

**Dose.** The interferon marker rises with dose and levels off between 20 and 40 µg (Figure 5). Manganese raises the ceiling of the response rather than its potency: the half-maximal dose is 13 µg with manganese and 11 µg without, while the fitted maximum is 58-fold against 16-fold. About 6.6 µg of PSLN-344 is predicted to match 20 µg of PSLN-212 on this marker [L59]. If protection tracks the marker, the protective threshold lies between the 17-fold that fails and the 37-fold that protects [L21], which corresponds to somewhere between about 7 and 20 µg: there may be little room to lower the dose and nothing to gain by raising it. Only a dose-ranging study with protection as the read-out can settle this.

## 5. Target demographic

**Primary target: adults aged 65 and over receiving seasonal influenza vaccination.** The benefit in our data is specific to aged hosts. Mice of 20 months or more correspond, by the usual approximation, to people in their sixties and older; that mapping is a convention, not something our data establish.

**For the TAM check, market research should size:**

- People aged 65 and over in the launch geographies, and the share vaccinated against influenza each year.
- Within that, the share already receiving an enhanced vaccine. That segment sets the realistic price reference and is the competition.
- The 50-to-64 band as an upside case only.
- Care-home residents and government pandemic-preparedness purchasing as separate, smaller lines.
- The route to market. PSLN-344 needs an antigen, so the unit sold is an adjuvanted intranasal vaccine dose, either with a vaccine partner or with our own antigen supply. The TAM should be built on doses, not on a stand-alone drug.
- A discount for antigen format. Everything so far used whole inactivated virus. If package 5 shows the benefit needs that format, the addressable share shrinks to manufacturers who make it.
- Annual re-dosing. The market is yearly, and repeat-dose safety is untested.

> **Outside our data (from general knowledge; please verify before use)**
>
> - High-dose, MF59-adjuvanted and recombinant influenza vaccines are the enhanced products already used in adults of 65 and over; any new product will be compared with them.
> - The licensed intranasal influenza vaccine is live-attenuated and is not indicated for older adults.
> - An earlier intranasal inactivated influenza vaccine with a bacterial-toxin adjuvant was withdrawn after cases of facial-nerve palsy. Regulators will expect nose-to-brain safety data, which is presumably why cholera toxin is the positive control in block S10-A.
> - Manganese is a recognised neurotoxicant on chronic exposure and can travel along the olfactory nerve after nasal exposure in rodents.
> - STING agonists can differ in activity between mouse and human STING, so activity in human cells cannot be assumed from mouse data.



## 6. Mechanism of action, from our data

**In one sentence:** PSLN-344 uses the lung's surfactant-uptake route to deliver a STING agonist with manganese to the alveolar compartment; in aged lungs this brings the early interferon-β response back to the young level and adds a GM-CSF signal that the manganese-free formulation does not produce; together these draw in CCR2⁺ monocytes and turn them into antigen-carrying dendritic cells; those cells prime cross-reactive CD8 T cells and tilt follicular T cells towards help, giving germinal centres and class-switched antibody; and the animals survive.

**What this chain does not yet establish**

- **That it runs through STING.** No STING-deficient host or STING antagonist was used. The only knockout in the file is of the interferon receptor, in donor marrow.
- **What manganese and the drug each contribute.** No block has a manganese-only or drug-free manganese liposome, and none pairs manganese with a different agonist. The only evidence that CDN-1807 itself matters is that it beats cGAMP in the manganese-free liposome (17- against 8-fold Ifnb1) [L57].
- **Which effector protects.** No CD8 depletion, no serum transfer and no mucosal IgA. The interventions stop at cell counts; none has survival as its read-out.
- **Full restoration of dendritic-cell activation.** CD40 and CD86 on lymph-node dendritic cells reach 11% and 15% against 39% and 38% in the young [L29a, L29b]. More cells arrive, but they are less activated: a residual deficit worth knowing about.
- **What happens to the macrophages.** As printed, inflammatory (Ly6C⁺CCR2⁺) macrophages are 5.6-fold fewer with PSLN-344 on day 5 (3.4 against 19.0 ×10⁵ cells) [L52a]. That would fit monocytes being diverted into dendritic cells, but it sits oddly with a monocyte influx twice as large and a 77-fold stronger Ccl2 signal [L24, L61], and the neighbouring block on the same sheet is mislabelled [L54]. I have not built on it [Q11].

**Four cautions.**

- At five animals per group almost every read-out separates protected from unprotected groups (27 of 33) [L66], so the present data cannot rank the markers. Markers and survival were also measured in different animals. Package 6 exists to settle which markers predict protection.
- The conventional human benchmark, an HAI titre of 1:40, did not signal protection here: 8 of 10 PSLN-212 sera reached it [L44], yet PSLN-212 survival was 9/73 [L02]. Every protected group was at 160 or above.
- One marker moved against the outcome over time. IFN-γ⁺ CD8 T cells were more frequent at day 60 than at day 14 (9.9% against 6.4%) [L33a, L33b] while survival was lower (14/21 against 41/45) [L11]. Frequency in the lymph node is therefore not, on its own, a measure of protection.
- No mucosal IgA was measured, which is a notable absence for an intranasal vaccine.

For clinical translation these lung and lymph-node read-outs need accessible counterparts, such as interferon-induced proteins in nasal fluid or blood within a day of dosing, activated circulating follicular helper T cells at about day 7, HAI and microneutralisation, and interferon-γ responses to conserved internal viral proteins. None of these has been bridged in our data.

## 10. Limits of this analysis

- I worked only from the two files. I had no protocols, raw instrument files, or records of randomisation or blinding; the annotation mentions neither.
- Groups are 3 to 10 animals. Of 43 contrasts between PSLN-344 and PSLN-212, 41 stay below 0.05 after false-discovery adjustment [L67], but any single read-out is modest evidence. The survival result rests on replication across experiments.
- Several readings rest on my inference from internal consistency: the identity of the second S09-A panel, the rotated headers, and treating constant terminal weights as animals that had died. The audit scripts reproduce each one; the data owners should confirm them.
- Statements marked as outside our data come from general knowledge and were not checked against sources here.
- I have no information on cost, timelines, intellectual property, competitors or the rest of the portfolio.

## Appendix C. Methods and files


### How the numbers were produced

- Parsing. Every block of the workbook is declared with explicit cell ranges in psln_blocks.py; a coverage check confirms that every non-empty cell is accounted for. The result is 6,120 observations, each with its cell address (13 of them form an unlabelled region on sheet S08 that is set aside); the remaining numeric cells are day, dose and time labels.
- Scales. Flow-cytometry percentages are analysed as they stand. qPCR fold-changes are analysed on the log10 scale and reported as geometric means. Antibody titres are analysed on the log2 scale and reported as geometric mean titres; HAI values printed as 0 are below the 1:10 starting dilution and are set to 5. Block S09-A is stored as log10 and is back-transformed for reporting.
- Tests. The methods stated in the annotation (one-way ANOVA with Tukey, two-way ANOVA, t test, log-rank) are reported alongside Welch t tests with confidence intervals, exact Mann-Whitney tests, Fisher exact tests, and log-rank tests with exact permutation p-values. Survival proportions carry Clopper-Pearson intervals. Pooled survival treats each experiment as a stratum (Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel); the interval for the pooled difference is Newcombe's.
- Body weights. Animals that leave a study appear either as blank cells or as a constant value repeated to day 14. Both are decoded (a terminal run of three or more identical values, or of two below 90%) so that dead animals are not averaged as if alive, and so that each weight table can be compared with its survival table.
- Share of the age gap closed. (PSLN-344 minus PSLN-212 in aged animals) divided by (young PSLN-212 minus aged PSLN-212), on the scale used for testing, with bootstrap intervals.
- Dose-response. fold = 1 + Emax x dose^h / (ED50^h + dose^h), fitted on the log scale with parametric-bootstrap intervals.
- Header screen. Because three blocks proved to carry wrong headers, every block containing aged PSLN-212, aged PSLN-344 and young PSLN-212 was screened for group order. PSLN-344 lies between the other two in 38 of 41 blocks; the exceptions are the two mislabelled ratio blocks and the 72-h interferon block [Q04].
- Ledger. build_ledger.py recomputes every cited number directly from the cited workbook cells, independently of the parser, and stops if any value disagrees with the analysis output.
